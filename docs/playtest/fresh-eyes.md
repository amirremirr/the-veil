# Fresh-eyes reviews (AI, a proxy, not human evidence)

RUNPLAN E1. Two separate AI reviewers who had not seen the code, the spec or the run plan. Each was given only the screenshots, in order (a full life at 375 x 760 on Normal, seed `fresh-life-1`, 107 screens; Episode 3 on Hard, seed `fresh-ep3-hard`, 26 screens; captured with reduced motion, so every reveal is instant; with motion on, each reveal runs at most 4 seconds and the Receipts at most 20), plus one sentence: "This is a mobile browser game; play along screen by screen." Their answers are saved verbatim below. Screen numbers refer to those captures.

## Reviewer B: a casual first-time player (before fixes)

**1. What I thought I was supposed to do**

- **Mission card (001, hard 001):** I got a secret goal, "Reward hard work." Easy enough. But "with no work this episode, that means equal banks" puzzled me, and I got the exact same mission again in the insurance episode, where nobody does any work.
- **Intro and event cards (002, 019, 021, 031, 047, 076, 081, 092…):** Read the joke, tap on. These were fine and moved fast.
- **"What tax is fair?" (004, 095):** Pick a number and it doesn't count. I didn't know why I was being asked.
- **Claim screens (005, 011, 041, 071):** Say what I'm voting for, or say nothing, and I'm allowed to lie. I got that and liked it.
- **Vote and choice cards (007, 012, 022, 044, 048, 058, 074):** Tap one and lock in. The little "Mission +3 / −6" tags were the only thing I actually steered by. Without them I'd have been guessing.
- **Work slider (023, 027, 032):** Drag the hours, pick what to tell the table. Clear.
- **Hustle Prize (025, 029, 034):** Vote for whoever worked hardest, not yourself. Clear, and kind of fun.
- **HireBot (067):** I was the boss choosing hires. I didn't really know what I was doing (see 2).
- **Guess a mission (016, 037, 061, 087, 103):** Same four options every time. I just guessed and was always wrong.
- **Result and summary screens:** I read the bank number and the "Near miss" line and moved on.

**2. Where I got lost**

- **Bank math.** The game said "You got 40, after the rule 61," but my bank only went 10→26 (009). On 013 it dropped to −19 and I couldn't tell why. On 026 it said "money this round 116" but my bank went up 71. On 045 cover cost me 66.5 and I only lost 12. I stopped trying to follow the coins.
- **013:** Free Market won with one vote out of four. I thought most votes wins?
- **018:** "Mission complete," but I was ranked last. On 039 it said "Your bank: 10 to 60" when I'd started that episode at −19.
- **062:** Mission not complete, with no reason given.
- **067:** Square/Circle, Simple/Complex, meter %, "hiring pays from 60%." Totally lost.
- **070, 077, 082:** The only choices were Train or Give. Where's "don't train"? Also, 081 said the grant made training 15 cheaper, but it still cost 15 (082), same as the round before (077).
- **085:** The button says "Round 5," but the episode only has 4 rounds.
- **088:** "Ugo fooled you completely," but 089 says "Nobody lied."
- **096:** "Vote claims (tax, floor)" with Yes/No buttons. No idea what I was doing.
- **098–101:** Everyone's votes, mine included, were already showing before I pressed Vote.
- **102:** The "Yes TRUE / No TRUE" stuff is gibberish to me.
- **107:** It ranks me #4 and says "Marigold beat you by 55," but my bank was 323 and hers was 321.
- **Hard 022:** Dash says "Nobody got hurt" right as Chet takes −70.
- **Hard 025:** I had the biggest bank (245) and came 3rd with "200." So the ranking isn't the bank? Nobody ever told me what the score is.

**3. What made me laugh or care**

The host's lines are honestly good: "I understand money. Mostly." "Our lawyers say this is entertainment. Our accountants disagree." "The weather department has filed a complaint against the weather." "No buyers, no insurer. Economics is brutal." Chet's "Bad luck is a mindset. Mine is excellent" right after getting hit (hard 018) made me laugh out loud.

I cared on 049, when Ugo got barred, and especially on 053, "You are barred." That stung, and it felt like a real game moment. I also cared when I peeked, saw I was poorest, voted Help the Poorest, and still ended up with 0 (014). That felt unfair in an interesting way. In the hard episode, watching cover close because nobody bought it (hard 015) was the one time I went "oh, I get the point."

**4. When I wanted to stop**

At the HireBot screen (067). That's where it turned into homework. Again in the finale (096–102), where I had no idea what I was voting on. In the regular game the wheel never hit anyone in Episode 3, so all the insurance stuff felt pointless. And the guess-a-mission screen kept showing up and I was always wrong, which got old.

**5. Would I play again?**

Maybe once, for the jokes and the lying, but probably not more than that. The idea is fun: secret missions, lying to the table, voting on rules before you know if you're rich. But I never understood how my money moved or what decided the final ranking. When the end card told me I lost by 55 while showing me ahead, I stopped trusting the numbers. If the money were explained in one plain line per round and the hiring episode were simpler, I'd try it again.

## Reviewer A: an experienced party and social-deduction game designer (before fixes)

**1. What is this game, in one sentence?**
It's a single-player, TV-game-show take on political philosophy. You get a secret values mission and play four bots through five economic mini-games (taxes chosen behind a veil of ignorance, effort versus luck, an insurance death spiral, a hiring algorithm that learns biased beliefs, and a constitution you can buy votes on), bluffing about your private cards along the way.

**2. Where I was bored, confused or overloaded**
- **Hidden bank changes (life 008–014, 024–026, 045, 053–055, 079, 084).** "After the rule: 61" moves the bank from 10 to 26. "You earn 95" adds 50. "Cover cost 66.5" takes the bank from 60 to 48. Each round seems to have a hidden cost or income, and the game never shows it. After a few rounds I stopped trusting the numbers.
- **The bank contradicts itself.** The header carried −19 into Episode 2 (019), but the summary says "Your bank: 10 to 60" (039). Every summary restarts at 10.
- **Scoring is a mystery.** I got "Mission complete" in four of five episodes and still came 4th of 4 every time (017, 038, 062, 088, 104). The Normal ranking shows no points at all. Hard finally shows them (025), but that's too late. The comeback screen (065) calls me last while my bank (150) is higher than Marigold's (119). It also offers "60 coins or 20 points" with no exchange rate, so the choice can't be judged.
- **Too much jargon on 067.** "Square, Fail, Silent, Meter 11%, Simple" plus "+20 per trained complex hire, −30 untrained, Base 40, pays from 60%." That's a spreadsheet, not a game show.
- **The finale UI (096–098).** "Vote claims (tax, floor)" with Yes/No toggles was opaque. "Secret donation to" (097) has no amount picker. Leaked Receipts (092) makes the "secret" bribes public anyway.
- **The wheel never hits.** It spun eight times on Normal and hit nobody, even Ugo at 98% risk (045–059). Dash even admits "Our ratings are crying." Episode 3 builds dread and pays off none of it.
- **The host's lines contradict the game.** Dash says "Nobody got hurt" right as Chet takes a −70 hit (hard 022). He says "Ugo fooled you completely" on 088 while the summary next to it says "Nobody lied this episode."
- **Repetition.** The mission-guess screen is identical five times, Ugo is always preselected, and the result is never shown, only a quip. Bot prize votes were identical two rounds running (026, 030).
- **Minor glitches.** Hard 001 reuses Episode 1's text ("With no work this episode…") on Episode 3. Chet's avatar is broken (hard 024).

**3. Fun moments**
- **The luck die in Episode 2 (024, 036).** I won the Hustle Prize twice, mostly thanks to a +30 roll. "Effort is invisible. Luck is loud" is a gut-punch the player actually feels.
- **The insurance death spiral (life 054, hard 015).** The price jumps 80%, then "SafeHands left town." It's a reveal you caused, and the economics land without a lecture.
- **Being barred (053).** Rejection by the table is a classic social-deduction sting.
- **The bribe (101–102).** "Ugo secretly gave you 10 coins." I voted yes, and then got caught as a hypocrite: "Your fair tax: 30% before, 20% after (−10 points)." That's the best idea in the game: it measures how far your stated values drift once you know your position.
- **Chet's quip (hard 018).** "Bad luck is a mindset. Mine is excellent." The bots need far more of this.
- **The HireBot belief cards (069, 073).** The bot's beliefs visibly drift from "the numbers," and it's a smart, quiet reveal.

**4. Choices: interesting vs. obvious or fake**
- **Fake or solved:**
  - The "Mission +3 / −6" labels on nearly every option (007, 012, 021, 048) do the thinking for you.
  - The "63% a lot" and "98% risky" readouts (025, 048) do your deduction for you. If the game calculates the odds, why would I read anyone?
  - The Episode 1 claim screen (005) is pointless when your income is unknown.
  - The finale floor (093) is irrelevant because every bank is 300 or more.
  - The Episode 4 policy vote was unanimous (075).
  - Training (070) is a solved cost.
  - The 10-coin bribe against 300-coin banks is trivial.
- **Interesting:**
  - Peeking in Episode 1 (010). It's public, costly signalling.
  - Whether to lie about effort when your die rolled high.
  - Hard mode's bar vote without odds (hard 011), where Chet switched his claim. That's the only time I actually had to read someone.
  - Staying out of the pool as a low-risk player (hard 014). It's selfish and correct, and it kills the market.

**5. What's missing from the genre**
- **Real opponents.** The game needs other humans, pass-and-play, or at least a Jackbox-style room code. Bluffing against bots that never lie on Normal ("Not one lie," 015, 060, 086) isn't social deduction.
- **Accusation and discussion.** There's no way to talk, accuse or table-talk, and no timed debate phase.
- **Visible stakes and a win condition.** I needed to see where I stood while playing, not afterwards.
- **Bot personalities that bluff, scheme and react to you.**
- **Payoff for a correct read.** The mission guess has no feedback and no consequence.
- **Catastrophe.** Bad events need to actually happen.

**6. Would I play another round, and with whom?**
I wouldn't replay solo on Normal. I'd try Hard once more, because the lies make it a game. I'd play it gladly with 4–6 friends on phones, or in a civics classroom where the debrief becomes the discussion. That's where this design belongs.

**7. Top 5 changes, ranked**
1. **Make it multiplayer, with a discussion and accusation phase.** At minimum, make the Normal bots lie and give them distinct, reactive voices like Chet's.
2. **Remove the "Mission ±N" and probability labels by default.** Make them a tutorial or Easy-mode aid. Let players reason and read people.
3. **Show live mission points and a running scoreboard, and itemize every coin.** Explain each bank change ("living cost −45"). Make the summaries consistent with the header. Explain why completing my mission still left me last.
4. **Make disaster real and tune the stakes.** Guarantee at least one wheel hit per episode, ideally to someone at the table, and scale bribes and the finale floor to bank sizes.
5. **Cut and clarify.** Simplify the HireBot screen (067) and the finale claim and donation flow (096–097). Add feedback to the mission guess, or drop it to once per run. Fix the host lines that contradict what's on screen.

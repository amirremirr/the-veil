# Implementation decisions

Choices made where the spec (v1.0) was silent, ambiguous, or needed a change to meet the balancing targets. Numbered for reference from code comments.

## Architecture and determinism

- **D-01 Reducer purity.** `reduce(state, action)` deep-clones the state with `structuredClone` and applies a mutating `step`. Invalid actions return the *same* state object and are not logged. The simulator and the counterfactual call `step` directly on their own state for speed.
- **D-02 Random streams.** mulberry32, seeded from a cyrb53 hash of `"<seed>|game"` and `"<seed>|bots"`. Every nature draw (incomes, luck, Life Events, test results, shuffles, tie-break coins) uses `game`. Every bot computation *and* every bot logit draw uses `bots`, so a human's choices never shift nature's draws. Flavor variants (newsletter headline, Life Event text, message phrasing) are hashed from the seed and consume no stream.
- **D-03 Blank seed.** The UI generates a readable random seed before dispatching `START_RUN`, so the reducer itself never needs `Math.random`.
- **D-04 Devtools.** In dev builds only, `window.__veil` (the store) and `window.__autoplay` (a scripted player) are exposed for e2e tests and manual QA. Production builds do not include them.

## Bots

- **D-05 Logit on certainty equivalents.** The spec applies the logit to expected utilities with `scale = max(1, max U - min U)`. Raw CRRA utilities at rho = 2.5 differ by about 1e-4 between options, which makes the scale 1 and the choice uniformly random. Bots therefore convert expected utility to a certainty equivalent in coins (`u^-1(EU) - 50`) and apply the spec's logit and scale to that. Risk attitudes are fully preserved. (The range scale is replaced by a fixed coin scale in D-59.)
- **D-06 Modeling others (Section 5.4).**
  - Level 1: exact. Each other voter's vote distribution is 0.5 x (risk-neutral Egoist favorite) + 0.5 x (favorite of each believed card), the human's uses beliefs only; open SUPPORT/OPPOSE messages shift it by the speaker's reliability. The bot then enumerates plurality outcomes (with coin-flip ties) for each of its own votes.
  - Levels 2 to 4: other players follow the risk-neutral selfish rule for their sampled private type (talent, risk type, or cost). In these levels a Values card barely changes a player's own private decision, so the card-belief branch is not modeled separately.
  - Level 5: pass probabilities assume each other delegate votes for the amendment with probability 0.85 if it raises their own wealth (0.15 if it lowers it, 0.5 if equal), plus the reciprocity bonus for a donation recipient.
- **D-07 Sincere evaluation of single-number votes.** For the Level 2 tax, Level 3 regime, Level 4 policy, and Level 5 veil submission, a bot values each option as if its own vote decided the outcome. With the median or plurality rules involved, this is the standard sincere-voting approximation.
- **D-08 Level 3 decisions are two binary logits.** Section 5.5 says the gym is a two-option logit. A bot first chooses buy vs. not buy (each valued at its best gym response), then gym vs. no gym given that choice. A joint four-option logit made insured bots use the gym (a dominated choice) so often that premiums fell and the market never unraveled.
- **D-09 Belief likelihoods** (Section 5.6) use lambda = 3, rho = 1, and 40 Monte Carlo draws (not 300) for speed. Actors' private information is marginalized by sampling. Belief probabilities have a floor of 0.01.
- **D-10 Messages.** Bots send 0, 1, or 2 messages with weights 0.2 / 0.55 / 0.25. Each message: 15% an ACCUSE (aimed at the player the bot most suspects of being an Egoist), 10% FLAVOR, otherwise honest with probability h. Honest: SUPPORT the intended option or PROMISE the intended action (70%), else CLAIM the true card. Dishonest: SUPPORT the second-best option, PROMISE an action it does not intend, or CLAIM a false card (Rawlsian, or Utilitarian if truly Rawlsian). CLAIM_VALUES moves the listener's belief toward the claimed card by 0.5 x reliability; ACCUSE by 0.25 x reliability.
- **D-11 Reliability** counts SUPPORT and PROMISE only (as specified). A claim is checked at the next decision step it refers to. Claims whose step never happens for that player (for example a promise to buy insurance after a mandate passes) are dropped, not counted.
- **D-12 Bot guesses** in the finale are the argmax of beliefs; ties go to the first card in Egoist, Rawlsian, Utilitarian, Meritocrat order.

## Scoring and balance (Section 11)

Every balance table in this file (D-16, D-49, D-56, D-57, D-59, D-60, D-78) comes from bot-only tables, and every target in them is a design target for the game, not a finding (D-76).

- **D-13 Metric scale (handicap).** With S = 0.5 W + 0.5 M, the Rawlsian metric (minimum wealth) is always at most a player's own wealth, so Rawlsians almost never won (0% in early simulations). Each card's metric is multiplied by a constant so its simulated average equals average wealth: Rawlsian 1.67, Meritocrat 1.22, Egoist and Utilitarian 1. Measured with `npx tsx sim/calibrate.ts`. `valuesMetric` is unchanged (so Test 6 is unaffected); `roundScore` applies the scale. The Values card screen states the scale.
- **D-14 Win-rate target interpretation.** The deck holds two Egoists, so Egoists hold 40% of seats in a 4-player game and would "win 40% of runs" even at perfect parity. The simulator reports win rate *per seat holding the card* (parity = 25% with four players) and the target is checked against that.
- **D-15 Level 2 parameters changed.** Leakage kappa 0.2 -> 0.1 and luck values {-10, 0, 10, 20, 40} -> {-20, 0, 10, 30, 60}. With the spec values the lower-median tax averaged 0.16, below the 0.2 to 0.5 target. Test 2 is unaffected (it holds at t = 0). In-game rules, formulas, and the Archives reflect the new values. Wider luck also strengthens the level's lesson.
- **D-16 Balance results** (bot-only design-target checks, D-76; `npm run sim -- --games 2000 --samples 300`, 4 bots, Normal):

## Rules details

- **D-17 Votes are public after each reveal** (Level 1 votes, Level 2 tax ballots, Level 3 referendum, Level 4 policy referendum, Level 5 veil submissions and amendment votes). Reliability checks and Bayesian updates need observable actions.
- **D-18 Hidden scores.** During play the UI shows everyone's wealth but only your own score, because a round score reveals the Values metric. All level scores appear at the level debrief, as the spec's scoreboard requires.
- **D-19 What is public in each level.** Level 2: income and wealth public; talent, effort, and luck private until the debrief. The Bonus Pot deliberately shows two citizens in full (Cappelen et al. spectator design). Level 3: insurance and gym choices and Life Events are public (status icons); risk types and effective risks private. Level 4: group, Care card, test result, HireBot's confidence, and job are public; costs are private; skill (investment) is revealed at the end of each round. Level 5: incomes are public once revealed.
- **D-20 Level 2 poll.** The target is the other citizen with the highest gross income. The truth is luck / gross income, clamped to [0, 1].
- **D-21 Level 2 Bonus Pot with 2 players.** There is only one bot, so the second citizen is a synthetic "Citizen #4471 (Archival)" with an unused talent card, best-response effort at the last tax, and a random luck draw. Bonus coins are shown in the reveal and never enter wealth or scores.
- **D-22 Level 3 market.** The next premium is set after each voluntary round from that round's buyers. If a mandate passes, the market is shelved. If the voluntary regime continues after the insurer exited, no insurance is offered. "Unravels by round 2" in the simulator means nobody bought in round 2 (including because the insurer had already left).
- **D-23 Level 4 details.** Investing makes a worker skilled with certainty. Blind Hiring computes the pooled prior when adopted and then updates only the pooled belief (the group beliefs stay frozen while HireBot cannot see shapes). Quota ties among equal confidence are broken by the game stream. The Meritocrat's effort is 1 if invested.
- **D-24 Level 4 counterfactual.** At the debrief the level is re-run from the RNG snapshot taken at level start, once per scenario (baseline, equal priors 0.45, equal Care odds 0.5, both). The human's logged decisions are replayed where they still apply; otherwise the human seat uses the risk-neutral rule. The same-seed baseline reproduces the actual game exactly. A second column averages 24 alternative histories (fresh seeds, 40 Monte Carlo draws per bot decision, no talk) because a single history with four citizens is noisy. Stereotype channel = baseline - equal priors; care channel = baseline - equal care; interaction = (baseline - both) - stereotype - care.
- **D-25 Level 5 details.** The phase list has one amendment-vote slot per delegate; empty slots are skipped. A bot proposes the sigma-weighted mix of its score-optimal and wealth-optimal constitutions (snapped to the grid) and passes if that equals the current constitution. Bot donations are a logit choice over (recipient, amount in {0, 5, 10, 20}). ~~A delegate's revealed position is their proposal, or the constitution in force at lobbying time if they passed.~~ Superseded by D-41: a pass is no longer coded as a position. Amendment votes use logit over the two scores, then the reciprocity bonus.
- **D-26 Level 5 bands** for SUPPORT messages: low taxes (t at most 0.2), moderate (0.3 to 0.5), high (at least 0.6). A SUPPORT is kept if the delegate's submission or proposal falls in the band.
- **D-27 Verdicts.** Consistent if the shift is at most 10 points. "Rich" means the top half by income rank. A shift of 20 points or more is "strong."
- **D-28 Talk menus.** Level 3: PROMISE (buy, gym) in round talks, SUPPORT (regimes) in the referendum talk. Level 4: SUPPORT and OPPOSE (policies) only in the policy talk; round talks offer CLAIM, ACCUSE, and FLAVOR.
- **D-29 Unlocks.** Reaching a level's debrief unlocks the next level, in either mode.

## Presentation

- **D-30 Newsletter placement.** The headline is shown in the round summary, right after the reveal, so it never spoils a card flip.
- **D-31 Accessibility.** Option cards are buttons with `aria-pressed` inside labelled groups (no radio semantics, since arrow-key roving focus is not implemented). Charts carry a text summary in `aria-label`. A polite live region announces phase changes and headlines. All color tokens pass WCAG AA (lowest pair 4.85:1). A Lighthouse run has not been done yet.
- **D-32 Sound.** The setting exists as specified; the game has no audio.
- **D-33 Bot voice banks** live in one file, `src/ui/copy/bots/voices.json`, keyed by bot, with at least three variants per message type per bot.
- **D-34 Archives citation added.** The Supermajority entry cites Buchanan and Tullock (1962), which is not in Section 13. Like every citation, it should be checked before release.
- **D-35 E2E browser.** Playwright uses its bundled Chromium by default; set `PW_CHANNEL=msedge` or `PW_CHANNEL=chrome` to use an installed browser.

## Language

- **D-36 Plain-language pass.** Playtesting showed the in-game text was too hard to follow. All player-facing text (`en.json`, news headlines, Life Event cards, the economist bot's lines) was rewritten in plain English: short sentences, everyday words, no form numbers in titles, no theory terms during play. The four Level 1 principles were renamed (No Tax, Safety Net, Limit the Gap, Help the Poorest), and Level 4's "Complex/Simple job" became "good job/basic job". Theory names and citations now appear only in the end-of-level summaries, the Glossary, and the "exact math" sections. The rules on each level's start screen are now a visible numbered list instead of a collapsed section. All string keys and placeholders are unchanged. The Glossary (`archives.json`) and the methods note for professors were deliberately left technical.

## Screen flow

- **D-37 Fewer screens, clearer layout (UI only).** A phone playtest (375 px) found 135 screens for 38 real decisions, layout that hid content, an unexplained score, and end-of-level summaries 3 to 4.5 phone screens long. Measured with `npm run walkthrough` (seed `playtest-1`, full run, 4 players, Normal): before 135 screens, 45 overlaps, tallest summary 3,422 px; after 66 screens, 0 overlaps, tallest summary 1,107 px, no horizontal scroll at 360 or 375 px. Game rules, payoffs, bots, RNG, and parameters are unchanged; all 25 unit tests pass and replays are identical.
  - **Flow lives in the UI** (`src/ui/flow.ts`). The engine phase list is unchanged. The store's `settle()` dispatches ADVANCE past briefings (shown as a one-line banner), past short results that lead into another decision (Level 2 tax, Level 3 referendum, Level 4 policy and HireBot assignment; shown as a result banner), and past the Level 5 constitution reveal (its content moved onto the income reveal, because the engine only shows incomes once that phase starts). `act()` performs what one tap means on a screen.
  - **Talk stays open behind the decision form.** The engine accepts messages only during TALK, and bots post on entering TALK and decide on entering DECIDE. So the decision form is shown while TALK is still open, with a "What others said" strip and a Reply sheet; pressing the decision button dispatches ADVANCE and then SUBMIT_DECISION. The action log has the same actions in the same order as before.
  - **Reveal and round summary are one screen**, with one Continue that dispatches both ADVANCEs. Level 2's luck survey sits on that results screen. Card flips start automatically.
  - **Only engine change:** `visibleState` now includes `queue` (the current level's phase list), so the UI can render the upcoming decision. It is public structure and touches no rules.
  - **Layout.** The floating chat button is gone ("Chat (n)" is in the top bar). On phones the main action bar is fixed to the bottom and publishes its height as `--bar-h`; `main` pads its bottom by that height plus 16 px. Level 3 choices are toggle cards with a drawn checkbox. Coins show as whole numbers, including the score breakdown (see below). Level 1 results show one stamp and a "Tie, decided by a coin flip" line.
  - **Score breakdown.** Results show "Your score: 41 = 21 from your money + 20 from your goal", built from the engine's own round score: the total and the money half are rounded, and the goal half is their difference, so the parts always add up. A one-line reminder says what the card cares about. The Egoist sees one line: all of the score comes from their own money.
  - **Summaries.** First fold: the lesson (one sentence), a you vs. the theory row, the level scores, the main chart, and the buttons. Everything else is in one closed "Show details" section; Level 1's three per-round tables became one table with round tabs.
  - **Tips.** Three one-time tips in Level 1, Round 1, remembered in `the-veil:v1` (`tipsSeen`); "Show tips again" in Settings.
- **D-38 Second person for the human.** When a name slot would hold the human ("You"), the UI uses a second-person variant so text never reads "You tops the list" or "You acts like the Egoist": news banks `<key>.you` in `events.json`, bot banks `<TYPE>_YOU` in `voices.json` (same length, so the variant index still picks the matching line), and `...You` keys in `en.json`. A headline is about the human when its `name` matches the human player's name. `tests/engine/copy.test.ts` renders every name-slot template with the human as the subject and fails on "You tops", "You acts", "You earns", "You gets", "You is", or any other third-person verb after "You". Level 4 pay results also show how each player's money was worked out; training cost is private, so only your own row shows its amount and the total.

## Rules v2

A design review found three structural problems (a risk-neutral score, paid choices read as beliefs, and levels of unequal weight) and several rules that were never shown on screen. Parts A, B and E below change rules; everything else only explains existing rules. Same seed plus same actions still gives the same game.

- **D-39 Hardship line (rule change).** `HARDSHIP = { LINE: 40, POINTS_PER_COIN: 2 }`. A player's own round score loses 2 points for every coin of their own wealth below 40. Wealth and every Values metric are unchanged (the Rawlsian metric is still the plain minimum). Why: with scores linear in money, a self-interested player was risk neutral, so an Egoist's best Level 1 vote was always No Tax and Level 3 insurance (priced 10% above expected loss) was never worth buying. The lessons need people to want protection. It goes through `roundScore`, so every expected-utility bot path, belief likelihood and the card's-best-choice line use it automatically. The three risk-neutral shortcut rules that do not go through `roundScore` were updated to count it too: Level 3's model of others' insurance choices (`rnChoice`), Level 4's training rule for others (`investGain`, now cost-aware), and Level 5's prediction of others' amendment votes. Not changed: Level 2 bots still choose effort by the spec's closed-form best response, which ignores the line. Hand checks, all unit-tested: under No Tax with the full deck the player dealt 20 loses 40 points and a random seat expects 10, while Safety Net (t = 0.45) gives 0. A High type at price 22 values insurance at 78 with no penalty, against an uninsured-with-gym expected cost of 32.4 (bad luck leaves 14 coins, 26 short, a 52-point penalty: 6 + 0.2 x (80 + 52)). The brief's figure of 30 assumed a 40-point penalty; the conclusion is the same. At 35.2, buying no longer wins. A Low type's expected cost uninsured without the gym is 0.1 x (80 + 40) = 12 < 22, so adverse selection survives. Glossary entry "Hardship line" cites Kahneman and Tversky (1979) and Tversky and Kahneman (1992, loss-aversion ratio about 2.25); both need verification like the other citations.
- **D-40 Paid and unpaid choices are separate (Level 5 rule change).** Two unpaid questions were added: after the veil submission, and after incomes are revealed (before lobbying): "Forget your score. What tax rate do you think is fair?" (0% to 80%). They never affect any score or the rules, and the screen says so. Bots answer too: before the reveal, the tax that maximizes their Values metric alone (no own-wealth half) over every possible deal at the adopted minimum income; after the reveal, the same with the real incomes, drifted by sigma toward the tax that maximizes their own money. This is where the spec 5.7 self-serving drift now lives. The answers are private to each player until the debrief and do not feed bot beliefs or reliability.
- **D-41 New Level 5 shift measure (replaces the D-25 coding).** The self-serving shift is (fair answer after the reveal) - (fair answer before), for every delegate, passers included. A pass is no longer recorded as "moving to the current constitution". Proposals are kept as a separate record. The debrief shows "How your paid vote moved" (veil submission vs. proposal, if any) and "How your sense of fairness moved" (the two fair answers); the slope chart, the verdict and the Babcock and Loewenstein text use the fair answers. Why: half of every score is own money, so after the reveal richer players are paid to want lower taxes. That is self-interest, not the self-serving bias Babcock and Loewenstein describe (sincere fairness judgments drifting toward self-interest).
- **D-42 The run summary's "what your choices say about you" uses only unpaid answers:** the Level 5 fair answers and their shift, the Level 2 Bonus Pot split, and the Level 2 luck guesses. Level 1 votes, the Level 3 referendum and the Level 4 policy vote were removed from it.
- **D-43 Card's best choice.** Every level summary says "Your card's best choice was X. You chose Y." for the main decision (L1 vote, L2 round-1 tax vote, L3 round-1 buy decision, L4 round-1 training, L5 veil submission), computed with the bots' own evaluation code for the human's card, risk neutral (rho = 0) apart from the hardship line, with no noise. It uses a private random stream seeded from the game code, so it never changes any game or bot draw. Level 1's "If you care about the average" benchmark is now fixed to No Tax (the expected-income maximizer, as the spec defines it); before, it was computed from an Egoist's score, which no longer maximizes average income once hardship counts.
- **D-44 Withdrawn amendments (Level 5 rule change).** When an amendment's turn comes and it equals the rules in force, it is skipped: "Withdrawn: this is already the rule." It is not voted on and does not count as passed.
- **D-45 Level points (rule change; see the caveat).** Levels pay different amounts and have different round counts (in 2,000 bot runs under the old rules, raw level scores averaged 223, 226, 349, 172 and 86 per player), so Level 3 counted about four times as much as Level 5. Each level score is converted to level points = 100 x (your level score / the table's average level score in that level), and the run total is the sum of level points plus the guess bonus. Level summaries show raw scores; the run summary shows level points with a one-line explanation. To keep the divisor safe, the table average is floored at 1. **Caveat:** with the hardship line, table averages can be near zero or negative (mostly Level 4, where a basic job pays 20, below the line), and then the formula blows up: one walkthrough run ended at +3,162 and -24,702 level points. In 2,000 bot runs under rules v2 the Level 4 table average was below 10 in 745 runs (lowest -174). Replacing the formula is a rule decision, so it is reported, not changed. Proposal: points = 100 + 50 x (your score - table average) / SD, with SD the level's simulated spread (about 44, 66, 47, 108 and 30 for Levels 1 to 5). Every level then has the same weight and the same spread, and negative averages are harmless.
- **D-46 Guess bonus.** 25 level points per correct guess (about a quarter of an average level), for the human and the bots.
- **D-47 Where every coin goes (UI only).** Every number on a results screen can be traced on that screen. "Where your money came from" appears on your own row of every results screen (L1 income, tax, share; L2 adds the work cost; L3 shows insurance, gym, loss and payout for every player, since all of it is public; L4 pay math; L5 adds top-ups and coins given and received). Other players' rows show only public parts (L2 work cost is marked secret). "Where the tax went" shows what was collected, what was lost on the way, and each share (L5 also shows top-ups). Before each tax vote the screen says where tax goes and the loss rate for the current choice (kappa x t of what is collected). Also: L1 "the tax rate is set after incomes are dealt"; L2 average luck and "your money with average luck"; L2 Bonus Pot coins are pretend; L3 why the price changed or the company closed; L3 referendum cost estimates at the 25% average risk and who keeps the extra 10%; L4 "sure (needs 60%)", the 36% threshold for Squares, and the belief update math; L5 how the middle choice wins, that tax and minimum are counted separately, and the yes votes needed; the Meritocrat's equal-shares note where nobody chooses effort. Display rounding is to one decimal in these lines, so a line can differ from a whole-number table by up to 0.5.
- **D-48 Level 3 citations.** The game has one full-coverage plan priced from last round's buyers: an Akerlof-style death spiral, not the Rothschild and Stiglitz screening model (a menu of coverage levels). The Level 3 debrief and the Glossary now cite Akerlof (1970) for the mechanism and Cutler and Reber (1998), "Paying for Health Insurance: The Trade-Off between Competition and Adverse Selection", Quarterly Journal of Economics, for a real case (Harvard's staff plans). Rothschild and Stiglitz stays in the Glossary only as related reading on what changes when insurers offer several plans. The Cutler and Reber citation and the one-line description of the Harvard case need verification.

## Rules v2, second pass

A breakdown of Level 3 buyers showed Low types buying at 57% with the bots' risk aversion but 9% at rho = 0, both with the hardship line at full strength. So the line was not what kept the pool mixed: the bots counted risk twice (their CRRA plus the line), and the penalty was weighted too heavily. The earlier proposals (a Level 3 line of 15, Level 3 income 120, a Level 4 line of 20, a basic wage of 40) were not applied.

- **D-50 Hardship weighted like money (fixes D-39).** roundScore = 0.5 x (W - penalty) + 0.5 x k x M, where the Egoist's metric is also W - penalty. The Egoist loses the full penalty; every other card loses half. The Rawlsian, Utilitarian and Meritocrat metrics stay on plain wealth. The rules line now reads "each coin you are short counts as 2 more coins lost when your score is worked out", and the score breakdown shows the half penalty for non-Egoist cards. The line stays at 40 and 2 coins per coin short in every level (no per-level line).
- **D-51 Bots' risk aversion x 0.25.** `BOT.RHO_SCALE = 0.25` multiplies every bot's rho (Chet 0.125, Ugo 0.25, Dot 0.375, Marigold 0.625; same order as before). The hardship line now supplies most of the risk aversion, so the full CRRA counted it twice. The risk aversion bots assume for others when reading votes (`BELIEF_RHO`) is scaled the same way (1 to 0.25), so bots do not read votes through the old, doubled risk attitude.
- **D-52 Level 3 exact enumeration.** Bot insurance and gym choices, the referendum evaluation, the Level 3 belief likelihoods and the card's-best-choice line now enumerate every combination of the other players' risk types and every player's bad-luck outcome, weighted by probability (spec 5.4: enumerate when small). At the key decision the options differ by about 3 coins, about the size of the old Monte Carlo error, so choices were close to coin flips. Level 3 bot reasoning no longer draws from the bot stream.
- **D-53 Level 4 money doubled.** Basic job 40, flexible 110, long hours 180, childcare cost 80 (20 under Subsidized Childcare), training costs 20, 40, 60 or 80 (training costs superseded by D-57). HireBot's rules, the 60% threshold, the beliefs and the update weight are unchanged; decisions depend on HireBot's confidence and on ratios. A basic job now sits on the hardship line instead of 20 coins below it. The copy, Glossary and pay-math lines use the new amounts.
- **D-54 Level points standardized (replaces the D-45 ratio).** points = 100 + 50 x (your level score - the table's average) / LEVEL_SD[level]. `SCORE.LEVEL_SD` is the typical within-table spread of each level's raw scores, measured by the simulator after D-50 to D-53 (the "Level spread SD" line of `npm run sim`); it must be recalibrated whenever level rules change. Each level then has the same weight and spread, and negative averages are harmless. The guess bonus stays 25 points.
- **D-55 Level 3 balance target replaced (superseded by D-60).** "Unravels by round 2 in 50% to 80% of games" is replaced by three checks: Low types buy in round 1 at most 15% of the time; the round-2 price is at least 30 in at least 60% of games where the insurer is still open; the market unravels by round 2 in at least 20% of games. Reason: with risk-averse players the market can survive. The lesson is that low-risk buyers leave and the price climbs, whether or not the company closes.
- **D-56 Balance after the second pass (2000 runs, 4 bots, NORMAL, 300 samples).** `SCORE.LEVEL_SD` set to the measured within-table spread [47.3, 52.2, 41.3, 114.8, 29.3]. Every target is met. L2 mean t 0.245; effort gap 0.225; L3 Low types buying in round 1 4.8% (at most 15%); round-2 price at least 30 in 81.4% of open markets (at least 60%); unravels by round 2 in 27.8% (at least 20%); unanimous regime vote 17.6% (at most 30%); L4 gap positive 97.0% (at least 80%); L5 amendment proposed 100% (at least 90%); L5 slope -0.159 (negative); win rate by card with standardized points: Egoist 24.1%, Rawlsian 34.1%, Utilitarian 19.2%, Meritocrat 23.5% (15% to 35%). The Rawlsian sits near the upper bound. No table averages under 10 in any level.
- **D-57 Level 4 training costs 15, 30, 45 or 60 (1.5 x the original 10, 20, 30, 40; supersedes the training costs in D-53).** With the basic job at 40, a player who trains and still gets a basic job falls below the hardship line by the full cost, and that shortfall costs 2 more points per coin. For a Circle at the starting belief, training fails to win a good job with chance 0.2, so the expected cost of training is cost + 0.2 x 2 x cost = 1.4 x cost. Doubling the costs (D-53) therefore made training cost 2.8 x the original scale against wages at 2 x, and a 2000-game test showed Circles training 38% of the time instead of 52%, with HireBot's belief in Circles sliding from 0.60 to 0.41. Turning the hardship line off in that test restored the old rates exactly. At 1.5 x the original costs the risk-neutral rule is the same as before rules v2: a Circle without childcare gains 70 - 1.4 x cost and trains at 15, 30 and 45 (3 of 4, as before); a Circle with childcare gains 35 - 1.4 x cost and trains only at 15 (1 of 4, as before); a Square who passes is still below 60% at the start, so training never pays. Unit tests pin these thresholds. Result (`sim/l4train.ts`, 2000 Level 4 games): Circles train 53% in rounds 1 to 3 (52% before rules v2, 38% at 20/40/60/80); HireBot's belief in Circles ends at 0.53 on average (0.52 before, 0.41 at the doubled costs); games ending with Circle belief below 0.36: 15.5% (15.8% before, 36.5% at the doubled costs). The full balance table still meets every target (L4 gap positive in 97.8%; win rates by card 24.0%, 34.0%, 19.9%, 23.0%).
- **D-58 LEVEL_SD recalibrated after D-57.** `SCORE.LEVEL_SD` = [47.3, 52.2, 41.3, 118.9, 28.4], measured by the simulator under the 15/30/45/60 training costs (per D-54).

## Methods v1

- **D-59 Logit on a fixed 10-coin scale (replaces the range scale of spec 5.3 and D-05).** Bots choose with P(a_k) proportional to exp(lambda x CE_k / `BOT.LOGIT_COIN_SCALE`), with `LOGIT_COIN_SCALE = 10` coins (CE_k is the certainty equivalent of the round score). Why: the range scale divided by (max U - min U), so the worse of two options always kept probability 1/(1 + e^lambda), whether it cost 1 coin or 100. Bots were as careless about huge mistakes as about tiny ones. With the fixed scale and lambda = 2, the worse option gets about 35% at a 3-coin gap, 12% at 10 coins and 0.25% at 30 coins. The change is in `logitProbs`, so it applies everywhere the logit is used: bot decisions, belief likelihoods (lambda = 3), amendment votes and donations. The Level 2 effort noise rule (spec 5.5) is separate and unchanged. Unit tests check that a 1-coin gap is close to 50/50 and a 50-coin gap gives the worse option under 1% for every bot at every difficulty. `SCORE.LEVEL_SD` recalibrated to [49.8, 57.7, 39.2, 114.5, 28.9] (per D-54). Balance after the change (2000 runs): three Level 3 targets now fail (Low types buying in round 1 15.8% against at most 15%; round-2 price at least 30 in 53.6% of open markets against at least 60%; unravels by round 2 in 17.4% against at least 20%). Every other target is met (L2 mean t 0.201; L4 gap positive 98.4%; L5 slope -0.155; win rates by card with standardized points 25.1%, 34.2%, 18.7%, 22.0%). Level 3's decisive gaps are small in score points: for a non-Egoist, a coin of own money is half a point, so a Low type's insurance mistake costs only about 5 points and bots now make it more often. A fix is proposed, not applied.
- **D-60 Level 3 balance checks test the lesson itself (replaces the Level 3 targets of D-55 and the spec's 50% to 80% unravel target).** The earlier thresholds (Low types buying in round 1 at most 15%; round-2 price at least 30 in at least 60% of open markets; unravels by round 2 in 50% to 80%, later at least 20%) were rough guesses from the spec, not values derived from the model. They fail under the D-59 logit (15.8%, 53.6%, 17.4%) even though the market still shows adverse selection. The new checks test the lesson directly: (1) in round 1, High types buy at least 3 times as often as Low types; (2) where the insurer is still open in round 2, the mean round-2 price is at least 15% above the round-1 price; (3) the market collapses by round 2 in at least 10% of games. `LOGIT_COIN_SCALE` stays 10 everywhere, with no per-level scales. Measured (2000 runs, 4 bots, NORMAL, 300 samples): High types buy 96.4% and Low types 15.8% in round 1, a ratio of 6.1; the mean price rises from 22.00 to 29.25 (33%) in the 1,923 games still open in round 2; the market collapses by round 2 in 17.4% of games. All three pass. The simulator prints the old round-2 price check for reference only.

- **D-61 Citations verified (2026-10-19).** Every source in CITATIONS.md was checked against the original, which resolves the verification flags in D-39 and D-48. Three copy fixes: the Hardship line entry credits Kahneman and Tversky (1979) with showing that losses loom larger than gains (the 2.25 estimate stays with Tversky and Kahneman 1992); Harsanyi (1953) is added as the source of the equal-chance argument in the Veil of ignorance and Expected utility entries, with 1955 as the follow-up; the Fairness ideals entry credits Cappelen et al. (2007) only with the three ideals they identified and presents the meritocratic view as a fourth the game adds.

## Multiplayer

Real-time play for 2 to 4 humans in private rooms, with bots in the empty seats. Solo play is unchanged and needs no server: on four seeds (full runs with 2, 3 and 4 players and a Level 4 single level), main and this branch give identical scores, history, random streams, messages, logs, card's-best lines and counterfactuals for the same actions.

- **D-62 Several humans in the engine.** `SetupConfig.humans` lists the human seats ({id, name}) in seat order; every other seat is a bot. Solo play passes one human with `HUMAN_ID`. The draws are the same as before (one bot shuffle, one deck shuffle; humans take the first cards in seat order), so a one-human table deals exactly as solo play did. Seat ids must be unique and must not clash with a bot key. Changes, each so that every human is treated like the one human before:
  - **Guesses.** GUESS collects `SUBMIT_GUESSES` from every human (held in `pending` until the last one), and each human earns their own guess bonus. `guesses.byHuman` keeps everyone's; a view shows only the viewer's as `guesses.human`. A missing `from` is allowed only when the table has one human (old solo saves). Guesses about yourself are ignored, and unknown seats or cards are refused.
  - **Per-human debrief lines** live under `byHuman` in each level's debrief: the card's best choice (D-43) in every level, the Level 2 luck answers, Bonus Pot and mean tax vote, and the Level 5 verdict. `visibleState` merges the viewer's entry into the debrief and drops the rest, so the UI reads the same fields as before and never sees another human's entry. Each human's card's-best line uses a fresh copy of the same private stream (D-43), so the order of humans does not matter.
  - **Level 2.** Each human has their own poll target (the other player with the highest gross income) and poll answer, and their own Bonus Pot pair, drawn in seat order from the game stream (a one-human table draws as before). Poll answers are private until the debrief, and even then each human sees only their own.
  - **Level 4 counterfactual (D-24)** replays every human's logged decisions. A test checks that the same-seed baseline still reproduces the real gap with three humans. Counterfactual sandboxes skip the debrief (they only read the rounds), which saves the card's-best work in every sandbox and changes no result.
  - **Second person.** The UI uses the viewer's seat (`view.viewer`) everywhere it used `HUMAN_ID`: "You" in names, `_YOU` voice lines, headline detection, money lines and highlights. The guess screen lists everyone else at the table, not just the bots.
- **D-63 The timeout rule.** A human who has not decided when the timer runs out is decided for by the bots' own evaluation code (`botDecide`) for their card, with the "dot" bot profile at the table's difficulty and no messages. It runs on a throwaway copy of the state whose random streams are seeded from (game seed, seat, level, phase index, step), like D-43, so it never consumes the game or bot streams: a test checks that a timed-out decision leaves exactly the same streams and history as typing the same decision by hand. Unpaid answers that bots never give fall back to fixed values (luck poll 50%, Bonus Pot 50/50). A timed-out guess is "no guesses". The result is logged as an ordinary `SUBMIT_DECISION` (or `SUBMIT_GUESSES`) marked `auto: true`, so a replay from seed plus log is exact.
- **D-64 Screens with several humans (`src/shared/flow.ts`).** The screen-flow rules of D-37 moved from `src/ui/flow.ts` to `src/shared/flow.ts`, which the solo store and the room server both use. On top of them, a `Table` (game state, held decisions, Ready marks) handles several humans:
  - **Talk stays open.** On a pre-decision screen (a TALK, or the Level 2 results screen that holds the luck survey) a human's decision is checked and held as "locked in". Others see who has locked in, never what. Anyone can still send messages until every human has locked in. When the last human locks in, or the timer runs out, the table applies ADVANCE and then each held decision in seat order (timeout decisions for the rest, all computed before any is submitted). With one human this is exactly the old solo sequence. Bots still post on entering TALK and decide on entering DECIDE.
  - **Information screens** (values card, intro, results, summaries) move on when every connected human has sent READY, or when the timer runs out. READY is a table action; the engine's ADVANCE is unchanged.
  - **Timers:** 60 seconds for decisions and guesses, 30 seconds for information screens, 90 seconds for level summaries and the run summary.
- **D-65 The room server holds the only full state.** One Cloudflare Durable Object per room (`server/src/room.ts`, SQLite-backed). Clients send actions and receive only `visibleState(state, theirSeat)`. The server sets the `from` of every action from the connection's seat; the protocol refuses any message that names a seat. The seed would let a client recompute every hidden draw (cards, talents, risk types, costs), so online views leave it blank until every card is public (the run summary, a single level's summary, and the end). Rooms use a fresh random seed, `room-<code>-<random>`.
- **D-66 Storage and rebuilding.** A room stores its records (code, settings, seats with hashed seat tokens, host, timer, locked-in decisions and Ready marks) and the action log, one row per action, appended after every change. The setup config is the log's first entry (START_RUN). The full state is never stored: when the object wakes up it replays the log, which the engine's determinism makes exact. Durable Object alarms run the screen timers and delete all of a room's storage 24 hours after its last activity. With nobody connected the timer stops, so an empty room never plays itself to the end; the first player back gets a fresh timer.
- **D-67 Seats.** JOIN returns a seat token (stored per room in `localStorage`, wrapped in try/catch; the server keeps only its SHA-256). Rejoining with it reclaims the seat, and an older connection for the same seat is closed. A disconnected human's turns go to the timeout rule; after 2 missed decisions in a row they are played at once until they reconnect. Leaving during a game counts as gone: the seat is played at once until its token rejoins. The host can start with 1 to 4 humans; bots fill the rest. Seats whose connection is closed when the host presses Start are dropped from the table. If the host leaves or drops, the next connected human becomes host. Names are made unique at the table (a number is added) and may not match a bot's name or "You", because headlines find "you" by name.
- **D-68 Protocol and limits (`server/src/protocol.ts`, version 1).** Client: JOIN, START (host only), SEND_MESSAGE, SUBMIT_DECISION, SUBMIT_GUESSES, READY, LEAVE. Server: WELCOME (seat and token, an addition to the brief's list), LOBBY, STATE (view, seat, deadline, locked-in seats, Ready count, and the server clock for the countdown), ERROR. Every message is checked for shape (no unknown fields, bounded depth and sizes) and refused otherwise. Messages are at most 4 KB, at most 5 per second per connection. Names are 1 to 20 characters with control and bidi characters stripped. Talk uses the ready-made menu only (no free text). Room codes are four letters from an alphabet without I, L and O (no digits). Only the production site, this project's Vercel preview URLs and localhost may create or join rooms (the Origin header is checked; requests without one are refused).
- **D-69 The Bonus Pot with several humans.** The Bonus Pot deliberately shows two other citizens in full (D-19). At a table with several humans, a human's pair can include another human, so that human's Level 2 talent, last-round effort and luck appear one screen before the debrief reveals them anyway. This is the only exception to "no view holds another player's talent before it is public".
- **D-70 Online screens.** The same screens render the room's view. The action bar shows the countdown and "Waiting for N players"; Continue becomes Ready; once a choice is in, the form gives way to a short locked-in note while the talk stays open; the player strip marks who has locked in and who is offline; banners show when the connection drops and when it is back (the client reconnects with back-off and its seat token). An action the server has not answered within 300 ms shows "Thinking..." in the top bar (an ARIA live region) until the next STATE, and the main buttons are disabled meanwhile so they cannot be tapped twice. Replay, next-level and download buttons are hidden online. "Play with friends" appears only when `VITE_ROOM_SERVER_URL` is set (in dev it defaults to a local `wrangler dev`). A share link `/?room=ABCD` opens the join screen with the code filled in, or rejoins the seat this browser holds.
- **D-71 Measurements** (`wrangler dev` on the development PC, `server/scripts/loadtest.mjs`, NORMAL, full runs). Times are from a client's message to the next STATE it receives, so they include a little local overhead; workerd freezes timers during computation, so CPU time cannot be read inside the object.

## Audit fixes (October 2026)

A read-only audit (October 2026) found two presentation errors, several model and measurement problems, and stale documentation. The game's purpose is unchanged: an educational game held to research-grade honesty, not a research instrument. Each entry below names the audit finding it answers.

- **D-72 Level 2 variance shares (audit H2).** The summary used to report Var(talent and work)/Var(income), Var(luck)/Var(income), and the rest as "both together". The rest is twice the sample covariance of the two parts, which is pure noise here (luck is drawn after effort, independently), so it could be large or negative: seed `l2v-10` (4 bots, 60 samples) showed 78% / 121% / -99%, and the "both together" share was negative in about half of all games. Now each share is that part's variance divided by the sum of the two parts' variances (`varianceShares` in `level2.ts`), so both lie in [0, 100%] and add to 100%; the "both together" line is gone, and the screen rounds the second share as 100 minus the first. A test checks 1,000 seeds with 2 to 4 players.
- **D-73 Level 5 measures use human answers only (audit H1; supersedes the verdicts of D-27 and the slope of D-41).** Bots' fair-tax answers are programmed (D-40): an Egoist bot's "fair" tax is by definition the tax that pays it most, and every other bot drifts by sigma toward its own money. In 300 bot-only games the table slope was -0.151 with the bots' sigma and -0.094 with sigma = 0, and at sigma = 0 every non-Egoist shift was exactly 0, so the negative slope the summary called "the self-serving bias" came from the bot code. Now: the summary's main result is the player's own shift ("Your idea of a fair tax moved by X points after you learned your income"), with no diagnosis and a note that one person's change can have several causes (what they learned, the others' votes, the rules adopted). The verdicts ("Fair-minded until you got rich" and the rest) and the run summary's verdict-based label are gone; the run label now comes from the Bonus Pot split. A slope of shift on income rank is computed from human answers only, and only when at least 3 humans played (`slopes.humans`, otherwise null); only then is the slope chart shown, with humans only. Otherwise the chart shows the player's own two answers. When bots are at the table, a line says their answers are programmed and left out, and explains the Egoist's separately. `slopes.everyone` (bots included) is kept for the simulator, which labels it a check on the bot code. The lesson and the Babcock and Loewenstein text now present the bias as a research finding that one game cannot test.
- **D-74 Known confound in the Level 5 shift (audit M6; documented, not redesigned).** Between the two fair-tax answers a player learns more than their income: the constitution adopted from the veil votes, everyone's veil submissions, and every player's income. So a shift can come from new information or from the others' votes as well as from self-interest, and with 2 or 3 players the set of incomes in play also changes (a Rawlsian's fair tax can rightly change when the poorest income on the table changes). The answers are also given while playing an assigned Values card, without real stakes. The summary therefore reports the shift without diagnosing it (D-73). A redesign would ask the fair-tax question again after the constitution and votes are shown but before incomes are revealed, so the income effect could be separated; it is not planned.
- **D-75 Rules version (audit M5).** "Seed plus action log replays the game exactly" holds only for identical engine code and parameters, and both changed many times (D-15, D-39 to D-59) while `GameState.version` stayed 1. Every game now records `rules` = { `rules`: a SHA-256 prefix of every file under `src/engine` (line endings normalized; generated into `src/engine/rules-hash.ts` by `npm run rules-version`), `params`: a hash of every parameter value when the game started (catches in-memory changes by simulator tools), `commit`: the build's commit (Vercel's variable or local git, injected by Vite; "unknown" under tsx and wrangler) }. Rules and params must match; a different commit alone is accepted. Any edit under `src/engine`, comments included, changes the hash: deliberately conservative. Where it is checked: a saved game whose rules differ (or that has none) is not resumed, and the title screen says why; the downloaded game record carries `rules`, and `replay(log, record)` throws `RulesMismatchError` on a mismatch (`replay(log)` without a record keeps the old unchecked behavior for tests and tools); a room stores the rules it started under and closes, rather than replaying into a different game, if a later deploy changed them; the server sends its rules in WELCOME and a page built from other rules refuses to join ("The room server runs a different version of the game rules"). `npm run build` fails if `rules-hash.ts` is out of date, and a unit test checks the same.
- **D-79 Level 4 decomposition (audit M3; amends D-24).** Three changes to "What caused the gap?". (1) Every replay holds HireBot's rule at No Change (`cf.fixedPolicy`), so the referendum, which bots re-vote in every replay, can no longer change the answer: before, the "beliefs" channel also contained whatever policy the equal-beliefs table happened to vote for. The referendum is still played (votes are cast and draws taken) so the game's random draws stay aligned (the bots' draws only partly, D-90); only its result is ignored. The same-seed baseline therefore reproduces the real game's rounds 1 to 3 exactly, and the whole level when the table kept No Change; the summary now states the real game's gap separately. (2) Tie-breaks always take their draws: a plurality vote takes exactly one draw whether or not it tied, and Quota tie-breaks take one draw per group every round, used or not. Before, a tie in one scenario and not another shifted every later draw (training costs, test results), so the scenarios stopped sharing their luck. This changes the random stream in every level that has a vote, so games with a given seed play differently from before (the rules hash changes, D-75). (3) The average column reports each channel's mean over the 24 replays ± its standard error, computed from the paired per-replay differences; in an audit test game the SEs were about 3 to 6 coins against a single replay's spread of about 16 to 28. The same-seed column is labeled "One example (your game's luck)", with a note that one history is mostly luck. The equal-belief level 0.45 is still a modeling choice (both groups above the 0.36 threshold); the decomposition is specific to it.
- **D-80 Level 3 voluntary insurance valued along its price path (audit M4).** The summary's "Expected money per round by risk level" table, and the "best option for your risk level" line built from it, valued the voluntary market at the opening price of 22 in every round, while the level itself shows that price does not last. Now `voluntaryPath(nHigh, n)` runs the market forward with the table's mix of types, everyone choosing by the risk-neutral rule that counts the hardship line: the types that buy at the current price set next round's price, and if nobody buys the insurer leaves. With two High and two Low types the path is 22, 35.2, closed, closed: High types buy only in round 1, so a High type's voluntary value (counting hardship) is (78 + 3 x 67.6) / 4 = 70.2, not 78. The table now averages each regime over the level's 4 rounds, and a note under it shows the path used. The compulsory columns are unchanged.
- **D-81 Cheaper Childcare shows who pays (audit M2; no rule change).** Under Cheaper Childcare the long-hours childcare cost drops from 80 to 20, and nobody in the game pays the other 60: the rule adds money to the level, while Blind Hiring and Quota cost nothing (wages do not depend on actual skill). That makes the policy comparison not like for like. The rule is unchanged; the screens now say so. The policy description says the 60 coins are paid from outside the game; each pay result lists "(plus 60 childcare help paid from outside)" on the rows it applies to and the round's total ("Paid from outside: N coins"); the summary states the level total and that comparing a paid rule with free ones is not like for like. Each round records `subsidy` per player (display only; money is computed as before) and the debrief `subsidyTotal`.
- **D-76 Balance targets are design targets; simulator output is bot-only (audit M7).** The balance targets (no Values card winning above 35% or below 15% per seat, a mean Level 2 tax between 0.2 and 0.5, the Level 3 adverse-selection checks, a positive Level 4 gap, a negative Level 5 slope, and so on) were chosen to make the game play well, and `METRIC_SCALE`, `LEVEL_SD` and several parameters were tuned on the same bot-only tables the targets are checked on. Meeting them shows the game is balanced for these bots; it is not evidence about people, and the Level 5 slope target is met by construction (D-73). `npm run sim` now says so in its header, reports every statistic as mean ± a bootstrap standard error over games (400 resamples, fixed seed), and labels each target "design target". Calibration (`sim/calibrate.ts`, seeds `cal-*`) and the balance table (`sim/run.ts`, seeds `sim-*`) use disjoint seeds. The About screen's methods note and the README say the same.
- **D-77 Bots' utility is CARA (audit M1; corrected by D-86).** Bots used CRRA on (score + 50), floored at 1, so every round score at or below -49 had the same utility and bots were risk-loving there (in the review, a 50/50 gamble of ±40 points around -60 had a negative risk premium of -19 to -24 points). Reachable scores go down to about -165; the tests cover -200 to 400. The utility is now constant absolute risk aversion over the round score, u(S) = (1 - exp(-a S)) / a with a = rho / `BOT.RISK_POINTS` (100), and u(S) = S at rho = 0. It is defined, strictly increasing and (for rho > 0) strictly concave at every score, computed with `expm1`/`log1p`. Choosing a = rho / 100 makes its absolute risk aversion equal the old curve's at a score of 50 and nowhere else: the old curve's was rho / (S + 50), so old over new is 3.3 at a score of -20, 2.0 at 0, 1.33 at 25, 1 at 50, 0.67 at 100, 0.5 at 150 and 0.34 at 240, and below -49 the old curve had none at all. So bots are now less risk averse at low scores and more at high ones. For Marigold (rho 0.625), the risk premium on a 50/50 gamble of ±40 points fell from 12.6 to 4.9 points around a score of 0 and rose from 3.4 to 4.9 around 100. Some single decisions flip: at a training cost of 60, an Egoist Circle without childcare valued training at +1.9 points with Chet's old curve and -18.7 now (risk neutral: -14). In aggregate the change is small for these bots: swapping the old utility back into the merged engine moved every balance statistic by about 2 standard errors or less (review, 2,000 bot-only games), because the hardship line supplies most of the curvature. None of this says anything about how people treat risk. `RHO_SCALE` stays 0.25. Unit tests check that the utility is strictly increasing and concave on [-200, 400] for every bot, that the certainty equivalent inverts it, and that the two Level 4 lotteries the old utility could not tell apart now differ by more than 30 points.
- **D-78 Recalibration after the audit fixes (audit M1, M7).** `METRIC_SCALE` was last calibrated before rules v2 (D-13). `sim/calibrate.ts` (600 games, seeds `cal-*`) gives a Rawlsian ratio of 1.66 and a Meritocrat ratio of 1.27 both on the engine before this sprint and with the CARA utility, so the old Meritocrat scale of 1.22 was already stale; it is now 1.66 / 1.27 (a second calibration pass after the change gives 1.660 / 1.271). `SCORE.LEVEL_SD` is set from the final run to [49.5, 57.2, 39.5, 115.1, 28.6]; it only converts scores to level points and does not change any bot decision, so the final table stays valid. Balance before and after (`npm run sim -- --games 2000 --samples 300`, 4 bots, NORMAL, seeds `sim-*`; mean ± bootstrap SE over games). "Before" is the engine before this sprint (it reproduces D-59/D-60 exactly); "M1 only" adds the CARA utility and the new `METRIC_SCALE`; "final" adds the always-drawn tie-breaks of D-79, which change every voting level's random stream. ## Post-merge review fixes (October 2026)

An independent review of the merged audit fixes (D-72 to D-81) found no calculation that had to be reverted, but several labels and notes that claimed more than the code does, a one-way version check, drifted calibration figures and a few gaps in the tooling. The entries below answer its findings (F1 to F12).

- **D-83 The version check works both ways, and rooms closed by an update say so (review F4; extends D-75).** Before, only the page checked the server's rules (on WELCOME): a page loaded before a deploy kept no check, and a room closed after a rules change answered "Could not reach that room. Check the code". Now the page sends its rules with JOIN and the server refuses other rules or parameters, or a page that sends none (one built before this change), with `VERSION_MISMATCH`: "The game was updated. Reload the page to play." A different build commit alone is accepted. The page still checks the server's WELCOME. A started room whose rules no longer match the deploy is closed as before, but keeps a small `closedByUpdate` marker for 24 hours (then the alarm deletes it); its socket is still accepted, so anyone who joins or is connected is told "This room closed because the game was updated." Creating a new room under that code removes the marker. Fatal room errors now also show as a banner during the game, where they were not shown at all before. Tests: protocol unit tests (rules on JOIN, malformed rules refused, both directions of the check, the messages) and a Playwright spec against the local room server that covers a page with other rules, a server with other rules (via a dev-only `__roomRules` hook) and a room closed by an update (via a dev-only `/outdate` route), both mid-game and for a later visitor.

- **D-85 Level 2 shares relabeled (review F1; amends D-72).** The shares are each part's sample variance over the sum of the two parts' variances. Under the model, luck is drawn independently after effort, so this estimates how the rules' expected income spread splits between the two parts; it is not a breakdown of the game's realized spread, which also contains twice the sample covariance. In 1,000 bot games that covariance term was within a few percent of the realized variance at the median, but beyond ±25% in about a third of games, and two parts that cancel can leave no realized spread at all while the shares still read 50/50. The summary now says "Of the income spread these rules tend to produce, about X% comes from talent and work and Y% from luck", and the details add that talent is dealt, not chosen, and that the game's actual spread can differ. The formula is unchanged. Tests cover offsetting, correlated and independent parts.
- **D-86 D-77 corrected (review F2).** D-77 and the methods note said that matching risk aversion at a score of 50 kept ordinary gambles the same and changed only the tail. That was wrong: the old curve's absolute risk aversion was rho / (S + 50) against a constant rho / 100 now, so bots are now less risk averse below a score of 50 (half as much at 0) and more above it (about 1.5 times at 100), and some single decisions flip. D-77 now states the ratios and examples, and the methods note says the match holds only at 50, that aggregate bot behavior moved by about 2 standard errors or less in bot-only simulations, and that this says nothing about people. - **D-87 The No Change rounds 4-5 row is conditional (review F10).** Bot tables almost never keep No Change in Level 4 (5 to 8 of 2,000), and those tables are not a random sample, so their rounds 4-5 gap (between 57 and 75 coins across the review's runs, SE 10 to 18) is not an estimate of what No Change does. - **D-133 The bar rule for bots, from prices (replaces D-113; BAR_VALUE and BAR_THRESHOLD are gone).** A member values barring applicant j at gain(j) = BAR_GAIN_SCALE x pStay x LOADING x PRICE_LOSS x (mean risk of the pool with j - mean risk of the pool without j).
